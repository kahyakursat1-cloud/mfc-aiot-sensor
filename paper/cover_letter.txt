Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled:

"A Simulation-Grounded Virtual Prototyping Framework for MFC-Powered AIoT
Environmental Sensor Nodes with Embedded Anomaly Detection"

for consideration as a Research Article in MDPI Sensors.

SCOPE FIT
This work sits at the intersection of the three application domains most
active in Sensors: energy-harvesting IoT hardware, embedded machine
learning (TinyML), and precision agriculture sensing. The manuscript
addresses the critical open problem of reliable pre-prototyping design
for battery-free sensor nodes -- a barrier that prevents the wider
adoption of microbial fuel cell (MFC) energy harvesting in field-deployed
WSN systems.

PRINCIPAL CONTRIBUTIONS
1. We present, to our knowledge, the first Simulation-Emulation-Prototype
   validation pipeline for MFC-powered AIoT nodes. A literature-calibrated
   substrate-depletion OCV emulation model bounds the energy simulation
   within a cross-study fidelity envelope of [-1.6%, +8.5%] MAPE,
   validated independently against Zhang et al. (Int. J. Mol. Sci. 2016).
   This directly addresses the simulation-only limitation that has
   historically restricted acceptance of pre-prototyping IoT studies.

2. An unsupervised Isolation Forest anomaly detector achieves
   F1 = 0.955 +/- 0.022 (5-fold CV, n = 50/class) without labelled
   training data; under realistic sensor noise and electrode drift,
   F1 = 0.880 and ROC-AUC = 0.929 -- bounds derived from four independent
   virtual validation experiments (Sec 5.7).

3. A compact Random Forest duty-cycle decision model reduces average
   energy consumption by 47.5% vs. a fixed-interval baseline, enabling
   self-sustaining operation within the MFC power envelope (<200 uW mean).

4. A 200-trial Monte Carlo simulation with hardware imperfections confirms
   75.0% energy-positive operation -- a conservative, hardware-realistic
   bound compared to the 81.5% parametric sweep.

NOVELTY RELATIVE TO PRIOR ART
The closest prior works -- Zheng et al. (Sensors 2015) and Zhang et al.
(IJMS 2016) -- demonstrated MFC-powered wireless sensing but used fixed
duty cycles with no embedded ML or adaptive control. Donovan et al.
(IEEE TPEL 2011) introduced programmable power supply MFC emulation for
testbed evaluation but without edge intelligence. This manuscript
integrates all three dimensions (energy harvesting, adaptive control, and
embedded anomaly detection) within a single reproducible framework, and
is the first to provide cross-study emulation fidelity evidence for
MFC-powered AIoT nodes.

SIMULATION-ONLY JUSTIFICATION
Stable soil MFC biofilm formation requires 2-6 weeks of in-situ
conditioning, making iterative pre-prototyping experiments impractical
at the design stage. The framework is explicitly positioned as a
pre-prototyping tool with a structured path to hardware validation;
physical benchtop construction using the fully described $40/node BOM
is identified as the immediate next step. All simulation parameters are
calibrated from peer-reviewed experimental measurements [1,3,5,6,11,25,26],
and the cross-study MAPE envelope confirms that the models accurately
reproduce the energetic dynamics of independent physical MFC systems.
